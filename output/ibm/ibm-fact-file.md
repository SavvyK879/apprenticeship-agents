## 1. Company Overview

IBM describes itself as bringing "together software, consulting and infrastructure to help clients solve the
business challenges that matter most" [1]. It states its purpose as "To be the catalyst that makes the world work
better" [2].

* **Sector:** enterprise technology and services — software, infrastructure, and consulting, with hybrid cloud
  (Red Hat) and AI (watsonx) as the two commercial anchors [1][3].
* **Size:** 300,000+ employees across 170+ countries, 660+ products, and 2,500+ businesses in its Partner Plus
  programme [1].
* **Direction of travel:** IBM guided to full-year constant-currency revenue growth of four to five percent in its
  Q2 2026 results, and expects free cash flow to rise by roughly $1 billion year over year [3]. watsonx, Red Hat,
  HashiCorp and Confluent were named as the software lines carrying that growth [3].
* **Quantum:** IBM has committed more than $10 billion to quantum over five years and says it remains on track to
  deliver the first large-scale fault-tolerant quantum computer by 2029 [3]. It completed its acquisition of HRL
  Laboratories in August 2026 to accelerate that work [4].
* **UK footprint:** UK apprentice locations are London, Manchester, Hursley (Winchester) and Warwick [9].

**Why this matters for an application:** IBM is not positioning itself as a consumer tech company. It sells to
enterprises and governments. Framing your interest around client problems and enterprise systems will land better
than framing it around consumer apps.

## 2. Values

IBM's three stated corporate values are [2]:

1. "Dedication to every client's success."
2. "Innovation that matters — for our company and for the world."
3. "Trust and personal responsibility in all relationships."

IBM also runs a set of "Growth Behaviors", which it describes as "all about 'how' we act and show up to drive
growth, innovation, and inclusion" [2]. The individual behaviours are not published on that page.

On what it wants from apprentices specifically, IBM says it looks for "eager and curious individuals who have a
passion for technology, an appetite for learning, and the determination to succeed" [5]. Elsewhere it states it is
"looking for individuals that have a passion for technology" [6]. The 2025 DTS listing made "keen interest and
passion in technology" an explicit requirement [9].

IBM frames its apprenticeship route around skills-first hiring: by "placing the focus on skills and competencies,
rather than the need for a traditional educational degree, the Apprenticeship program helps to close the tech
skills gap" [6].

**Reading the values as an applicant (unverified opinion, not an IBM statement):** the three values map almost
directly onto interview competencies. "Dedication to every client's success" is the client-focus competency,
"innovation that matters" is the initiative/problem-solving one, and "trust and personal responsibility" is the
integrity and ownership one. Preparing one story per value is a reasonable way to cover the ground.

## 4. Role Requirements

**Programme:** Level 6 Digital and Technology Solutions Degree Apprenticeship. Apprentices choose one of five
specialisms: Business Analysis, IT Consultancy, Cyber Security, **Software Engineering**, or Data Analysis [7].

**Award:** Level 6 professional qualification plus a BSc (Hons) in Digital and Technology Solutions [7].

**Duration:** four years [7].

**Study pattern:** apprentices spend roughly 20% of their time on university study. The University of Exeter
delivers this in "week-long blocks over the term, rather than one or two days per week", so that apprentices based
far from campus can take part [7]. IBM's university partners for DTS have included the University of Exeter,
Manchester Metropolitan University, the Open University [10] and Northeastern University London [13].

**Locations (2025 cycle):** Hursley, London, Manchester, Warwick. Minimum three days a week on site, and applicants
must live within commutable distance of one of them [9].

**Salary (2025 cycle):** £26,000 starting salary [9]. The 2026 Technology Sales Level 6 apprenticeship, a sibling
programme, advertised £27,000 [14].

**Entry requirements (2025 cycle listing) [9]:**

* Minimum 120 UCAS points (equivalent to BBB at A level)
* GCSE Maths and English at grade 4 or above, or equivalent
* Valid and eligible residency status and the right to work in England, without needing sponsorship
* Further Education completed before the start date
* Must not have already started or completed a similar apprenticeship or university course
* "keen interest and passion in technology"
* Mobility, as the roles are client-facing

**What apprentices do:** apply university theory to real client problems from the start. Exeter's description is
that the programme "enables me to apply the skills I'm learning to real client problems, driving greater value for
them and expanding my own skillset" [7], and that apprentices learn "the foundations of IT and coding as well as
how to apply project management frameworks" [7]. The 2025 DTS listing described placements spanning data and AI
technical sales, artificial intelligence, governance, business analytics and business automation, with technical
exposure to databases, data lakes, warehouses, large language models and business automation [9].

**Career outcome:** IBM frames each pathway as providing "everything you need to start your career in the tech
industry and get the necessary exposure to set you up for a lifelong career" [5].

## 5. Application Process Steps

**Timing.** Note this section covers the **DTS** apprenticeship cycle. The Full Stack listing Savya saw ran on its
own timetable, which is not recorded here because the listing is gone. Do not assume the dates below applied to it.

The February 2026 DTS window recruited the **2026** intake, which starts before A levels finish for anyone sitting
them in summer 2027. The relevant window is the next one. IBM's
register-your-interest material states start dates are available for **September 2027 onwards** [11], and the 2026
window opened **13 February 2026** and ran for roughly one week [11]. So the working assumption is a window around
**February 2027** for a September 2027 start.

This is an assumption about the pattern, not a published date. Register on IBM's talent network now so the opening
is pushed to you rather than discovered late [12].

Stages, based on published descriptions of recent IBM UK apprenticeship cycles [11]:

1. **Short initial application.** A quick first-pass form.
2. **Online cognitive ability test.** Sat remotely.
3. **Full application form.** The longer written stage, where competency and motivation answers are given.
4. **Exploration session (assessment centre).** Includes a group discussion exercise and one or more interviews.
   Candidates also meet current IBM apprentices at this stage.
5. **Final matching.** Candidates are matched to a specific role and business area.

**Two practical points:**

* The 2025 DTS listing closed at "Midnight Sunday 17th August 2025" with a start date of Monday 6 October 2025 [9],
  so IBM has run more than one intake pattern. Do not assume a single annual window.
* The 2026 round reportedly had technical problems on opening [11], and IBM reopened a sibling 2026 apprenticeship
  after A Level results day [14]. Check the listing more than once.

**Unverified opinion:** a one-week application window plus a cognitive test as stage two means speed matters more
here than at most employers. Having the application answers drafted before the window opens is likely to be worth
more than polishing them after.

## 6. Application Tailoring Tips

**Mirror IBM's own language.** Three phrases appear repeatedly in IBM's own material and are worth echoing in the
written application: "passion for technology" [6][9], "appetite for learning" [5], and "client's success" [2]. IBM
asks for a "keen interest and passion in technology" as a listed requirement [9], so this is a box being ticked,
not a throwaway line — evidence it with something concrete.

**Lead with self-directed technical learning, not classroom grades.** IBM's stated position is skills over
credentials [6][5]. The grades get you past the 120 UCAS threshold [9]; what differentiates is domain knowledge
acquired outside a syllabus. Anything built, broken, or taught yourself belongs near the top.

**Frame everything around a client or a user.** "Dedication to every client's success" is the first value listed
[2], and the 2025 listing states the roles are client-facing and require mobility [9]. A project story that ends
with what changed for the person using it will fit IBM better than one that ends at the technical achievement.

**Prepare for a group exercise, not just an interview.** The exploration session includes a group discussion [11].
This is where "trust and personal responsibility in all relationships" [2] gets observed. Bringing others into the
discussion is usually scored better than dominating it.

**Pick the Software Engineering specialism deliberately and say why.** There are five specialisms [7]. Naming
Software Engineering and explaining the choice against the other four shows you read the programme rather than the
brand.

**Connect to what IBM is actually selling right now.** watsonx, Red Hat and hybrid cloud are the named growth lines
[3], and quantum is the long-horizon bet at $10bn over five years [3]. Referencing enterprise AI or hybrid cloud is
more relevant than referencing consumer AI products.

**Likely interview questions to prepare (unverified opinion, inferred from the values and the stated process):**

* Tell us about a time you put someone else's needs ahead of your own preferred approach. *(client success)*
* Describe something technical you taught yourself, and why. *(passion for technology, appetite for learning)*
* Tell us about a time you made a mistake and what you did about it. *(trust and personal responsibility)*
* Why an apprenticeship rather than a full-time degree? *(a standard screen for this route)*
* Why IBM rather than another tech employer? *(enterprise vs consumer framing matters here)*
* What technology development in the last year interested you most, and why?

**Things to avoid:** IBM is an enterprise business [1]. Positioning yourself purely around consumer apps, games, or
startups without connecting back to business or client outcomes will read as a poor fit.
